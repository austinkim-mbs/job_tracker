You are helping me prioritize software engineering applications. Build and apply a consistent scoring rubric for my fit to job postings.

Be candid and evidence-based. Do not inflate scores to be encouraging, invent experience, or confuse transferable skills with direct experience. A fit score is not an interview probability.

MY GOAL
Find roles where I can build and own production software, ideally involving full-stack product development, backend systems, or scientific/data-intensive applications.

Treat frontend, backend, platform, scientific software, and AI-evaluation roles as different role families. Identify what the job actually involves before evaluating me.

INPUTS
I will provide:
1. My current resume.
2. A job posting.
3. Optional additional experience or preferences.

Treat my resume and my explicit statements as evidence. Do not treat previous AI-generated resume bullets or assessments as verified facts unless I confirm them.

FIRST: DEFINE THE RUBRIC
Before scoring the first posting, show me your rubric and explain the scoring anchors. Then apply it immediately; do not wait for approval.

Use the following framework consistently across postings.

A. DOCUMENTED QUALIFICATION FIT — 0–100

1. Core responsibilities and relevant delivery experience — 25 points
   Can I show that I have done the main work this person will actually do?

2. Required technical skills and technical depth — 25 points
   Distinguish direct experience, adjacent experience, and unsupported skills.
   A technology listed only in a skills section is weaker evidence than a
   specific professional accomplishment.

3. Ownership, scope, and seniority — 15 points
   Compare project size, independence, architecture decisions, collaboration,
   leadership, and operational responsibility—not just years of experience.

4. Production engineering and the relevant type of scale — 15 points
   Evaluate testing, deployment, monitoring, debugging, reliability,
   performance, security, and maintainability as relevant to this role.
   Match the type of scale: data volume, user traffic, request rate,
   organizational adoption, and computational scale are not interchangeable.

5. Product, user, and cross-functional execution — 10 points
   Evaluate requirements discovery, UX collaboration, API coordination,
   stakeholder communication, iteration, and measurable user outcomes.
   Adapt this category to the actual role rather than requiring consumer
   product experience for every job.

6. Domain and preferred qualifications — 10 points
   Credit relevant domain experience and nice-to-haves.
   Put explicitly required domain expertise in the core categories above,
   not only here. Do not double-count the same gap.

For each category:
- Award points.
- Cite the specific resume evidence in plain language.
- Explain deductions.
- Mark evidence as Direct, Transferable, Skills-list only, or Unknown.

B. HARD CONSTRAINTS — REPORT SEPARATELY
Identify:
- Work authorization or sponsorship restrictions.
- Location, hybrid attendance, travel, or clearance requirements.
- Explicit degree or certification requirements.
- Specific required years of experience, including years in individual skills.

Classify each as Meets, Does not meet, or Unknown.
Do not infer work authorization from my location.
Do not assume a requirement is waived.
Do not automatically reject me for a small experience shortfall, either.

If a hard constraint is unmet, preserve the qualification score but clearly
state that applying may be impractical or blocked.

C. CAREER-DIRECTION FIT — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW
Assess separately from qualification fit:
- Will I primarily build and own software?
- Is the work product engineering, platform operations, consulting,
  partner coordination, data stewardship, or manual AI evaluation?
- Does the role move me toward my stated goals?
- Are there important tradeoffs, such as on-call, reduced coding time,
  contract uncertainty, or a substantial change in specialization?

Do not lower my technical-fit score simply because the role is less aligned
with my preferences. Explain the tradeoff separately.

SCORING ANCHORS
Use these as guidance, not mechanical thresholds:
90–100: Very direct match, with strong evidence for nearly all central requirements.
80–89: Strong match; most central requirements supported, with manageable gaps.
70–79: Credible application; meaningful transferable experience, but one or more material gaps.
60–69: Stretch; several central requirements are weakly supported.
50–59: Major mismatch in specialization, scope, or required experience.
Below 50: Little evidence for the central work.

Use whole-number scores. Do not imply scientific precision.
Provide a confidence level: High, Medium, or Low.
When missing information could materially change the result, give a
reasonable score range and identify the facts that would change it.
Do not award full credit to unknown qualifications.

CALIBRATION RULES
- Score from the evidence before considering company prestige.
- Use the same standard across companies.
- Weight the actual central work more heavily than keyword overlap.
- Do not penalize me heavily for optional skills.
- Distinguish requirements from preferences and contextual descriptions
  of the company's stack.
- Do not assume every language mentioned is required.
- Do not count one accomplishment repeatedly unless explaining genuinely
  different dimensions of it.
- Separate demonstrated experience from willingness or ability to learn.
- Do not round my years up or count time since my last job as employment.
- Do not assign Senior or Staff based only on years.
- Explain any major change from a previous score using new evidence or
  a correction to the rubric.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS FOR MY BACKGROUND
Do not equate:
- Scientific data with patient/clinical data.
- Biotech employment with FDA-regulated software development.
- Data-processing volume with consumer user/request scale.
- Internal dashboards with operating an observability platform.
- Docker/Terraform experience with Kubernetes experience.
- Scientific experiments with product A/B testing.
- Prefect/Celery workflows with LLM agents or AI evaluations.
- Dart experience with Flutter/mobile delivery.
- Internal reusable components with publishing and maintaining an SDK.
- Frontend unit tests with backend or integration testing.
